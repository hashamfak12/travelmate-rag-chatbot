São Paulo, Brazil's largest city and economic hub, is a vibrant metropolis known for its diverse culture, excellent food scene, and dynamic arts and nightlife.

Airports:
São Paulo is served by two main airports: Guarulhos International Airport (GRU), located about 25 km northeast of the city center, and Congonhas Airport (CGH), located about 8 km south and primarily serving domestic flights.

Transportation from airports:
From Guarulhos (GRU), the Airport Express Service (Aeroporto-Expresso) bus connects to various parts of the city, including the city center and major hotels, for around 42 BRL. Taxis have fixed rates to different zones, typically 120-180 BRL to the city center. Uber is widely available and often cheaper. The metro connection is being expanded.

From Congonhas (CGH), taxis cost around 40-60 BRL to the city center. The airport is closer and more convenient for city access.

Getting around São Paulo:
São Paulo's metro system is extensive and efficient, with six lines covering much of the city. A single journey costs 4.40 BRL. The Bilhete Único (Single Ticket) card can be used on metro, buses, and trains, with integrated transfers. The system is generally safe and well-maintained.

Buses complement the metro network and cover areas not served by the metro. They use the same Bilhete Único system.

Taxis are plentiful and metered. They're relatively affordable, with starting fares around 5 BRL plus distance charges. Uber is very popular and often more convenient.

The city is large and spread out, so walking between neighborhoods isn't always practical. Traffic can be heavy, so metro is often faster than road transport.

Major attractions:
The São Paulo Museum of Art (MASP) is one of Latin America's most important art museums, with an impressive collection of European and Brazilian art. The building itself is an architectural landmark, suspended above a plaza. Admission is around 50 BRL, with free entry on Tuesdays.

Ibirapuera Park is São Paulo's equivalent to Central Park, offering green space, museums, and recreational facilities. It's a great place to escape the urban hustle. The park is free to enter.

The Municipal Market (Mercado Municipal) is a food hall selling fresh produce, meats, cheeses, and prepared foods. It's a great place to sample Brazilian foods and the famous mortadella sandwich.

Liberdade is São Paulo's Japanese neighborhood, the largest Japanese community outside Japan. It offers Japanese restaurants, shops, and a weekend street market. The area reflects the city's diverse immigrant communities.

Vila Madalena is a bohemian neighborhood known for its street art, galleries, bars, and restaurants. It's a great area for exploring and nightlife.

The São Paulo Cathedral (Catedral da Sé) is a neo-Gothic cathedral in the city center. It's free to visit and impressive both inside and out.

Avenida Paulista is the city's main business and cultural artery, lined with skyscrapers, shops, restaurants, and cultural centers. It's closed to traffic on Sundays, becoming a pedestrian zone.

Accommodation:
São Paulo offers accommodation for all budgets. Hostels start around 50-80 BRL per night. Budget hotels cost 150-250 BRL, mid-range hotels 250-500 BRL, and luxury hotels can exceed 800 BRL per night.

Popular areas to stay include Jardins (upscale, good restaurants), Vila Madalena (bohemian, nightlife), Centro (central, historic), and Itaim Bibi (business district, modern).

Food and dining:
São Paulo is a food paradise, with cuisine from around the world reflecting its diverse population. Brazilian cuisine includes feijoada (black bean stew with pork), pão de açúcar (cheese bread), and churrasco (Brazilian barbecue).

A meal at a casual restaurant typically costs 40-80 BRL. Fine dining restaurants can cost 150-300+ BRL per person. The city has excellent Italian, Japanese, Middle Eastern, and other international cuisines.

Street food includes pastel (fried pastry with fillings), coxinha (chicken croquette), and acarajé (fried bean fritter). These typically cost 5-15 BRL.

Rodízio restaurants (all-you-can-eat) are popular, especially for churrasco and pizza. They typically cost 60-120 BRL per person.

Practical tips:
São Paulo uses Brazilian Reais (BRL). Credit cards are widely accepted, and contactless payment is common. ATMs are available but may charge fees for foreign cards.

The best times to visit are March to May and September to November when weather is pleasant. Summer (December-February) can be hot and rainy, while winter (June-August) is mild but can be cool.

São Paulo is generally safe in tourist areas, but be cautious, especially at night. Avoid displaying valuables, be aware of your surroundings, and use common sense. Some areas should be avoided, especially at night.

Traffic can be extremely heavy, especially during rush hours. The metro is often faster than road transport. Allow plenty of time for travel.

Tipping is expected: 10% at restaurants (often included in the bill), and small tips for services are appreciated.

Portuguese is the language, though English is spoken in tourist areas and hotels. Learning a few basic phrases is helpful.

The city is large and spread out. Plan your itinerary to minimize travel between distant neighborhoods.

Many museums and attractions offer free or discounted entry on certain days. Check individual websites for details.

Nightlife is vibrant, with many bars, clubs, and live music venues. The city has a reputation for late nights, with many places staying open until early morning.

Be cautious with street food if you're not used to it. Stick to busy stalls with high turnover. Drink bottled water.
