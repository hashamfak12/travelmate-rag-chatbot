Mumbai, formerly Bombay, is India's financial capital and largest city, known for Bollywood, colonial architecture, and vibrant street life.

Airports:
Mumbai is served by Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport (BOM), located about 30 km north of the city center. It's one of India's busiest airports, with both domestic and international terminals.

Transportation from airport:
The airport is connected by various transport options. Pre-paid taxis are available and recommended to avoid overcharging. Taxis cost approximately 500-800 INR to the city center, depending on destination. Uber and Ola (Indian ride-sharing) are widely used and often cheaper. Airport buses connect to various parts of the city for around 100-200 INR. The metro connection to the airport is being expanded.

Getting around Mumbai:
Mumbai's local train network is the lifeline of the city, with Western, Central, and Harbour lines. It's one of the busiest rail systems in the world. First-class and second-class tickets are available, with first-class being more comfortable but 8-10 times more expensive. Trains can be extremely crowded during rush hours.

The Mumbai Metro is expanding and provides an alternative to the local trains for certain routes. Fares are reasonable, typically 10-40 INR depending on distance.

Buses are extensive and cover areas not served by trains. They're cheap but can be crowded. BEST (Brihanmumbai Electric Supply and Transport) operates the bus network.

Taxis (black and yellow) are metered, though meters may not always be used. Negotiate fares in advance or use the meter. Auto-rickshaws are available in the suburbs and are cheaper than taxis. Uber and Ola are popular and often more convenient.

Ferries connect the city to Elephanta Island and other destinations.

Major attractions:
The Gateway of India is Mumbai's most iconic monument, built to commemorate the visit of King George V. It's located on the waterfront and is free to visit. Boat trips to Elephanta Island depart from nearby.

Elephanta Island, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is home to ancient cave temples dedicated to Shiva. The island is reached by ferry from the Gateway of India. The journey takes about an hour, and entry to the caves costs around 40 INR for Indians, 600 INR for foreigners.

Marine Drive, also known as the Queen's Necklace due to its curved shape and lights at night, is a beautiful 3.6 km promenade along the Arabian Sea. It's a popular spot for evening walks.

The Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Vastu Sangrahalaya (formerly Prince of Wales Museum) houses an excellent collection of Indian art and artifacts. Admission is around 85 INR for Indians, 500 INR for foreigners.

Crawford Market is a bustling market selling everything from fruits and vegetables to household goods. It's a great place to experience local life.

Dharavi, one of Asia's largest slums, offers organized tours that provide insight into the area's industries and community life. These tours are controversial but can be educational if done ethically.

The Bandra-Worli Sea Link is an impressive cable-stayed bridge connecting the suburbs to South Mumbai. It's best viewed from the Worli side.

Accommodation:
Mumbai offers accommodation for all budgets, though prices are high by Indian standards. Budget hotels and guesthouses start around 1,000-2,000 INR per night. Mid-range hotels cost 3,000-6,000 INR, while luxury hotels can exceed 10,000 INR per night.

Popular areas to stay include Colaba (tourist-friendly, near Gateway of India), Fort (business district, colonial architecture), and Bandra (suburban, trendy, good restaurants).

Food and dining:
Mumbai is a food paradise, especially for street food. Vada pav (spicy potato fritter in a bun) is Mumbai's signature street food, typically costing 10-20 INR. Pav bhaji, bhel puri, pani puri, and dosas are other popular street foods.

The city offers excellent regional Indian cuisines, from South Indian to North Indian to Maharashtrian. A meal at a local restaurant typically costs 200-500 INR. Fine dining restaurants can cost 1,500-3,000+ INR per person.

Seafood is excellent, with fresh fish and prawns. The city is known for its fish curry and rice.

Irani cafes are a Mumbai institution, serving chai (tea), bun maska (bread and butter), and other simple fare in a nostalgic atmosphere.

Practical tips:
Mumbai uses Indian Rupees (INR). Cash is still widely used, though credit cards are accepted at hotels, restaurants, and larger establishments. ATMs are everywhere.

The best times to visit are November to February when weather is pleasant (20-30°C). March to May is hot and humid, while June to September is monsoon season with heavy rains.

Mumbai is generally safe, but be cautious of pickpockets in crowded areas. Keep valuables secure and be aware of your surroundings, especially at night.

Traffic can be terrible, especially during rush hours. Allow plenty of time for travel. The local trains are often faster than road transport during peak hours, though extremely crowded.

Bargaining is expected at markets and with auto-rickshaw drivers, but not at restaurants or shops with fixed prices.

Dress modestly, especially when visiting religious sites. Cover shoulders and knees at temples and mosques.

Many attractions have separate pricing for Indian and foreign visitors. Carry identification.

The city never sleeps, with many restaurants and shops open late. Street food vendors operate into the early morning hours.

Tipping is appreciated but not always expected. 10% at restaurants is common. Small tips (10-20 INR) for services are appreciated.

Be cautious with street food if you're not used to Indian food. Start with cooked items and avoid raw vegetables and water from unknown sources. Drink bottled water.
