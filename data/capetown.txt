Cape Town, South Africa's legislative capital, is one of the world's most beautiful cities, nestled between Table Mountain and the sea.

Airports:
Cape Town is served by Cape Town International Airport (CPT), located about 20 km east of the city center. It's South Africa's second-busiest airport and handles both domestic and international flights.

Transportation from airport:
The MyCiti bus connects the airport to the city center and various destinations for around 75 ZAR. Airport shuttles are available, typically costing 150-250 ZAR. Taxis and Uber are available, costing approximately 250-400 ZAR to the city center, depending on destination. Car rental is also an option.

Getting around Cape Town:
Cape Town's public transport includes buses, minibus taxis, and the MyCiti bus rapid transit system. The MyCiti system is modern and efficient, with routes covering the city center, Atlantic seaboard, and airport. A myconnect card is needed, which can be purchased at stations.

Minibus taxis are the most common form of transport for many locals but can be challenging for tourists due to informal routes and safety concerns. They're very cheap but not recommended for first-time visitors.

Taxis and Uber are widely available and relatively affordable. Uber is particularly popular and convenient. Metered taxis are also available.

Car rental is a popular option for exploring the wider Cape Town area, including the Cape Peninsula and wine regions. Driving is on the left side of the road.

Major attractions:
Table Mountain is Cape Town's iconic flat-topped mountain, offering stunning views of the city and coastline. You can hike up (2-3 hours) or take the cable car (around 360 ZAR round trip for adults). Weather can change quickly, so check conditions before going. Advance booking for the cable car is recommended.

Robben Island, where Nelson Mandela was imprisoned, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Tours depart from the V&A Waterfront and include a ferry ride and guided tour. Tickets cost around 600 ZAR and should be booked in advance, especially in peak season.

The V&A Waterfront is a popular shopping, dining, and entertainment area with beautiful harbor views. It's a great place to spend time, with markets, restaurants, and the Two Oceans Aquarium.

Cape Point and the Cape of Good Hope are at the tip of the Cape Peninsula, about an hour's drive from the city. The area offers dramatic scenery, hiking trails, and wildlife viewing. Entry is around 376 ZAR per person.

Kirstenbosch National Botanical Garden is one of the world's great botanical gardens, set against the eastern slopes of Table Mountain. It's beautiful year-round and offers concerts in summer. Admission is around 75 ZAR.

Boulders Beach is home to a colony of African penguins. You can view them from boardwalks (around 176 ZAR) or swim at the beach (separate fee). It's a unique and charming experience.

The District Six Museum tells the story of forced removals during apartheid. It's a moving and important museum. Admission is around 45 ZAR.

Accommodation:
Cape Town offers accommodation for all budgets. Hostels start around 200-300 ZAR per night. Budget hotels cost 600-1,000 ZAR, mid-range hotels 1,000-2,500 ZAR, and luxury hotels can exceed 3,500 ZAR per night.

Popular areas to stay include the V&A Waterfront (convenient, tourist-friendly), City Bowl (central, good restaurants), Camps Bay (beachside, upscale), and Green Point (central, good value).

Food and dining:
Cape Town has an excellent food scene, with diverse cuisine reflecting its multicultural heritage. South African cuisine includes braai (barbecue), bobotie (spiced meat dish), and biltong (dried meat).

A meal at a casual restaurant typically costs 150-300 ZAR. Fine dining restaurants can cost 500-1,000+ ZAR per person. The city has excellent seafood, especially line fish and snoek.

The V&A Food Market and other markets offer diverse food options. Street food includes boerewors (sausage) rolls and samoosas.

Wine is excellent and affordable, with many excellent local wines available at restaurants and wine bars.

Practical tips:
Cape Town uses South African Rand (ZAR). Credit cards are widely accepted. ATMs are available, but be cautious and use those in secure locations.

The best times to visit are October to April (spring and summer) when weather is warm and pleasant. Winter (June-August) is mild but can be rainy and windy. Peak season is December-January (summer holidays).

Cape Town is generally safe in tourist areas, but be cautious, especially at night. Don't walk alone at night, avoid displaying valuables, and be aware of your surroundings. Some areas should be avoided. Consult locals or your accommodation for advice.

The sun is very strong. Always wear sunscreen, a hat, and sunglasses when outdoors, even on cloudy days.

Tipping is expected: 10-15% at restaurants, and small tips for services are appreciated.

Many attractions can be booked online in advance, which is recommended, especially in peak season.

The city is spread out, and having a car or using Uber/taxis is often necessary to reach attractions outside the city center.

Water safety: While tap water is generally safe to drink in Cape Town, some prefer bottled water. Be cautious at beaches due to strong currents and cold water.

Load shedding (scheduled power outages) can occur. Check schedules and be prepared with backup power for devices if needed.
