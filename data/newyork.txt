New York City, the Big Apple, is a vibrant metropolis known for its iconic skyline, world-class museums, diverse neighborhoods, and endless entertainment options.

Airports:
New York City is served by three main airports: John F. Kennedy (JFK), LaGuardia (LGA), and Newark Liberty (EWR). JFK is the largest, located in Queens about 26 km southeast of Manhattan. LaGuardia is closest to Manhattan, about 13 km northeast, and primarily serves domestic flights. Newark is in New Jersey, about 24 km southwest of Manhattan.

Transportation from airports:
From JFK, the AirTrain connects to the subway system. Take AirTrain to Jamaica Station (about 8 minutes, 8.25 dollars) and transfer to the E, J, or Z subway lines, or to Howard Beach for the A line. Total time to Manhattan is about 60-75 minutes. The Long Island Rail Road (LIRR) from Jamaica to Penn Station is faster (about 20 minutes) but costs around 10.75 dollars plus AirTrain fare. Airport buses like NYC Airporter cost around 20 dollars to midtown. Taxis and ride-shares have a flat rate of 70 dollars to Manhattan (plus tolls and tip).

From LaGuardia (LGA), the best option is usually a taxi or ride-share (20-40 dollars depending on destination) as public transport connections are less convenient. The M60 bus connects to the subway but takes longer.

From Newark (EWR), the AirTrain connects to NJ Transit trains to Penn Station (about 25 minutes, 15.25 dollars total including AirTrain). Airport buses cost around 18 dollars. Taxis cost 50-80 dollars plus tolls.

Getting around New York:
The New York City subway is extensive and operates 24/7, though service can be reduced late at night. A single ride costs 2.90 dollars. The MetroCard can be purchased at stations, or you can use OMNY contactless payment (tap your credit card or phone). Unlimited 7-day passes cost 34 dollars and are worth it if you'll be using the subway frequently.

Buses complement the subway network and use the same fare system. They're useful for crosstown travel where subway coverage is limited.

Taxis are iconic yellow cabs, with fares starting at 3 dollars plus distance and time charges. Uber and Lyft are widely available and often competitive with taxis.

Walking is a great way to explore Manhattan, especially in areas like Midtown, the Village, and SoHo where attractions are close together.

Citi Bike is the bike-sharing system, with stations throughout the city. A single ride costs 4.49 dollars for 30 minutes, or you can get a day pass.

Major attractions:
Times Square is the iconic crossroads of the world, though it's very touristy and crowded. It's worth seeing, especially at night when the neon signs light up.

Central Park is an 843-acre oasis in the middle of Manhattan. Highlights include the Central Park Zoo, Bethesda Fountain, Strawberry Fields (John Lennon memorial), and the Great Lawn. It's free to enter and perfect for walking, jogging, or relaxing.

The Statue of Liberty and Ellis Island are accessible by ferry from Battery Park. Ferry tickets cost around 24 dollars. Advance booking is recommended. The crown and pedestal require separate reservations.

The Empire State Building offers stunning views from its 86th and 102nd floor observatories. Tickets start around 44 dollars, with express options available for higher prices. The Top of the Rock at Rockefeller Center offers arguably better views and is less crowded, with tickets starting around 40 dollars.

The Metropolitan Museum of Art (the Met) is one of the world's greatest art museums. Suggested admission is 30 dollars for adults (you can pay less, but the full amount supports the museum). The Met Cloisters in Upper Manhattan focuses on medieval art.

The Museum of Modern Art (MoMA) houses an incredible collection of modern and contemporary art. Tickets cost 30 dollars for adults.

The 9/11 Memorial and Museum is a moving tribute to the victims of the September 11 attacks. Museum admission is 33 dollars, while the memorial (the reflecting pools) is free.

Brooklyn Bridge is an iconic suspension bridge connecting Manhattan and Brooklyn. Walking across (about 1.5 km) offers great views of the skyline. It's free and best done early in the morning to avoid crowds.

The High Line is an elevated park built on a former railway line. It's free and offers unique views of the city and Hudson River.

Accommodation:
New York hotels are expensive. Budget options are limited, with hostels starting around 40-60 dollars per night. Budget hotels cost 100-150 dollars, mid-range hotels 150-300 dollars, and luxury hotels can exceed 400 dollars per night.

Popular areas to stay include Midtown (convenient but touristy), the Upper West Side (residential, near Central Park), SoHo (trendy, great for shopping), and Brooklyn (often more affordable, with great food and culture).

Food and dining:
New York's food scene is world-class and incredibly diverse. Pizza is iconic - try a slice from a classic pizzeria (3-5 dollars) or visit famous spots like Lombardi's or Di Fara. Bagels with cream cheese and lox are a New York classic.

Street food includes hot dogs (2-3 dollars), pretzels, and halal carts offering chicken and rice platters (6-8 dollars).

Deli sandwiches, especially pastrami on rye from places like Katz's Delicatessen, are legendary but can cost 20-30 dollars.

Fine dining options are endless, with restaurants ranging from affordable ethnic cuisine (15-25 dollars) to Michelin-starred experiences (200+ dollars per person).

Food markets like Chelsea Market and Smorgasburg (weekend food market in Brooklyn) offer diverse options.

Practical tips:
New York uses US Dollars. Credit cards are widely accepted, though cash is still common at smaller establishments.

New York is generally safe, but be aware of your surroundings, especially at night and in less touristy areas. Keep valuables secure and be cautious of pickpockets in crowded areas.

Tipping is expected: 15-20% at restaurants, 1-2 dollars per drink at bars, and 15-20% for taxis.

The best times to visit are spring (April-June) and fall (September-November) when weather is pleasant. Summer can be hot and humid, while winter is cold but offers holiday decorations and fewer crowds.

Many museums offer free or pay-what-you-wish admission on certain days. The Met, for example, has suggested admission (you can pay less), while MoMA offers free admission on the first Friday of each month (4-8 PM).

Broadway shows are a must-see. Book tickets in advance for popular shows, or try the TKTS booths in Times Square, South Street Seaport, or Lincoln Center for same-day discounted tickets.

Subway etiquette: let people exit before boarding, move to the center of the car, and don't block doors. Avoid eye contact and keep conversations quiet.

Many attractions offer skip-the-line or express tickets for higher prices, which can be worth it during peak times to save hours of waiting.

New York is a walking city - comfortable shoes are essential. Distances can be deceiving, so check maps before walking long distances.
