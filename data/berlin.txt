Berlin, Germany's capital, is a city of contrasts where history, culture, and cutting-edge creativity converge.

Airports:
Berlin is served by Berlin Brandenburg Airport (BER), which replaced the old Tegel and Schönefeld airports. It's located about 18 km southeast of the city center. The airport handles both domestic and international flights.

Transportation from airport:
The Airport Express (FEX) train connects the airport to central Berlin in about 30 minutes for around 3.80 euros. Regional trains (RE and RB) also serve the airport. Airport buses are available. Taxis cost approximately 35-50 euros to the city center. Uber and other ride-sharing services are available.

Getting around Berlin:
Berlin's public transport system (BVG) includes U-Bahn (subway), S-Bahn (urban rail), buses, and trams. A single ticket costs 3.50 euros for zones AB (most of the city). Day tickets cost 9.90 euros, and weekly tickets are available. The Berlin WelcomeCard offers unlimited travel plus discounts to attractions.

The system is extensive and efficient, covering the entire city. Tickets must be validated before use (stamp them in the machines on platforms or in buses).

Biking is very popular in Berlin, with extensive bike lanes and bike-sharing programs. It's a great way to explore the city.

Taxis are available and relatively affordable compared to other major cities. Uber and other ride-sharing services operate.

Major attractions:
The Brandenburg Gate is Berlin's most iconic landmark, a symbol of German reunification. It's free to visit and located on Pariser Platz.

The Berlin Wall Memorial (Gedenkstätte Berliner Mauer) provides insight into the division of the city. The outdoor exhibition is free. The Documentation Center offers more detailed information.

The East Side Gallery is a 1.3 km section of the Berlin Wall covered in murals by artists from around the world. It's the longest open-air gallery in the world and free to visit.

The Reichstag Building houses the German parliament. The glass dome offers excellent views of the city and is free to visit, but advance registration is required (book online well in advance).

Museum Island (Museumsinsel) is a UNESCO World Heritage Site with five world-class museums, including the Pergamon Museum and the Neues Museum (home to the bust of Nefertiti). A day ticket for all museums costs around 19 euros.

The Holocaust Memorial (Memorial to the Murdered Jews of Europe) is a powerful and moving monument. The underground information center is free but requires advance booking.

Checkpoint Charlie was the famous border crossing between East and West Berlin. The area is touristy but historically significant. A museum nearby tells the story of escape attempts.

The Berlin Cathedral (Berliner Dom) is an impressive Protestant cathedral. Admission to the cathedral and dome costs around 10 euros.

Accommodation:
Berlin offers good value accommodation compared to other European capitals. Hostels start around 20-35 euros per night. Budget hotels cost 60-100 euros, mid-range hotels 100-200 euros, and luxury hotels can exceed 250 euros per night.

Popular areas to stay include Mitte (central, historic), Kreuzberg (alternative, nightlife), Prenzlauer Berg (trendy, family-friendly), and Charlottenburg (upscale, quieter).

Food and dining:
Berlin's food scene is diverse and excellent. Traditional German food includes currywurst (sausage with curry ketchup), schnitzel, and various regional specialties. Doner kebab, invented in Berlin, is ubiquitous and delicious, typically costing 4-6 euros.

A meal at a casual restaurant typically costs 12-25 euros. Fine dining restaurants can cost 50-150+ euros per person. The city has excellent international cuisine, especially Turkish, Vietnamese, and Middle Eastern.

Street food markets are popular, offering diverse options. Markthalle Neun hosts a street food Thursday with vendors from around the world.

Beer culture is strong, with many excellent local breweries and beer gardens. A beer typically costs 3-5 euros.

Practical tips:
Berlin uses Euros. Credit cards are widely accepted, though cash is still common, especially at smaller establishments. Many places prefer cash.

The best times to visit are May to September when weather is pleasant. Spring and fall are also nice. Winter can be cold but offers Christmas markets.

Berlin is generally safe, though be cautious in some areas at night, especially in the eastern parts of the city. Use common sense and be aware of your surroundings.

Tipping is customary: round up or leave 10% at restaurants. Service charges are usually included, but additional tips are appreciated.

Many museums offer free entry on certain days (often the first Sunday of the month) or reduced prices. The Museum Pass Berlin offers access to many museums for around 29 euros for 3 days.

The city is spread out, so plan your itinerary to minimize travel between distant neighborhoods. Public transport is efficient but can take time.

English is widely spoken, especially in tourist areas and by younger people. However, learning a few German phrases is appreciated.

Many attractions require advance booking, especially in peak season. Book online when possible.

Berlin has a vibrant nightlife scene, with many clubs, bars, and live music venues. The city is known for its electronic music scene.
