London, the capital of England and the United Kingdom, is a historic and vibrant city with world-class museums, theaters, and diverse neighborhoods.

Airports:
London is served by six airports, with Heathrow (LHR) and Gatwick (LGW) being the main international hubs. Heathrow is the busiest, located about 24 km west of central London. Gatwick is about 45 km south. Other airports include Stansted, Luton, City, and Southend.

Transportation from airports:
From Heathrow (LHR), the Heathrow Express is the fastest option, reaching Paddington Station in just 15 minutes. Tickets cost around 25 pounds for a single journey if booked in advance, or 37 pounds on the day. The Elizabeth Line (formerly Crossrail) also serves Heathrow, taking about 30-35 minutes to central London for around 12-15 pounds. The Piccadilly Line on the London Underground takes about 50-60 minutes but is cheaper at around 6 pounds. Taxis cost approximately 50-90 pounds to central London.

From Gatwick (LGW), the Gatwick Express reaches Victoria Station in about 30 minutes for around 20 pounds. Southern Railway offers a slightly slower but cheaper alternative. Airport buses are available but take longer.

Getting around London:
The London Underground, known as "the Tube," is extensive and covers most of the city. A single journey within zones 1-2 costs 2.80-3.40 pounds depending on time of day. The Oyster card is a smart card that caps daily fares, making it economical for multiple journeys. Contactless payment cards work the same way and are often more convenient for visitors.

The bus network is comprehensive and offers great views of the city. A single bus journey costs 1.75 pounds with Oyster or contactless. The famous red double-decker buses are iconic.

The Elizabeth Line provides fast connections across central London and to Heathrow Airport.

Black cabs are iconic but expensive, with fares starting around 3-4 pounds plus distance charges. Uber and other ride-sharing services are widely available.

Walking is a great way to explore central London, with many attractions within walking distance of each other.

Major attractions:
The British Museum houses one of the world's greatest collections of art and artifacts, including the Rosetta Stone and the Elgin Marbles. Admission is free, though special exhibitions require tickets.

The Tower of London is a historic castle where the Crown Jewels are kept. Tickets cost around 33 pounds for adults. Be prepared for crowds, especially in summer.

Westminster Abbey is a stunning Gothic church and the site of royal coronations. Tickets cost around 27 pounds. The adjacent Houses of Parliament and Big Ben are iconic landmarks.

The London Eye offers panoramic views of the city. Tickets start around 32 pounds, with fast-track options available for higher prices.

Buckingham Palace is the monarch's official residence. The State Rooms are open to visitors in summer (July-September) for around 33 pounds. The Changing of the Guard ceremony takes place on certain days and is free to watch.

The British Library, Natural History Museum, Science Museum, and Victoria and Albert Museum are all world-class and offer free admission to permanent collections.

Covent Garden is a vibrant area with street performers, shops, and restaurants. The nearby Royal Opera House is world-renowned.

Camden Market offers eclectic shopping, street food, and a unique atmosphere.

Accommodation:
London has accommodation for all budgets, though prices are generally high. Hostels start around 20-40 pounds per night. Budget hotels cost 60-100 pounds, mid-range hotels 100-200 pounds, and luxury hotels can exceed 300 pounds per night.

Popular areas to stay include Covent Garden, South Kensington, Westminster, and King's Cross, each offering different experiences and price points.

Food and dining:
London's food scene is incredibly diverse, reflecting its multicultural population. Traditional British fare includes fish and chips, full English breakfast, and Sunday roast. A meal at a pub typically costs 12-20 pounds.

The city offers cuisine from around the world. Borough Market is a foodie paradise with stalls selling everything from artisanal cheese to international street food.

Afternoon tea is a British tradition, with options ranging from 25-80 pounds depending on the venue. High-end hotels like The Ritz and The Savoy offer luxurious experiences.

Street food markets like Borough Market, Camden Market, and Spitalfields Market offer affordable and delicious options.

Practical tips:
London uses the British Pound (GBP). Credit and debit cards are widely accepted, and contactless payment is common.

The weather is famously changeable. Always carry an umbrella and dress in layers, even in summer.

London is generally safe, but be aware of pickpockets in crowded tourist areas and on public transport.

Many museums and galleries offer free admission, though donations are appreciated. Special exhibitions usually require paid tickets.

The London Pass can save money if you plan to visit many paid attractions, but calculate if it's worth it for your itinerary.

The best times to visit are late spring (May-June) and early fall (September-October) when weather is pleasant and crowds are manageable. Summer is peak season with higher prices and more tourists.

Booking attractions in advance online often saves money and time, avoiding queues.

Tipping is customary in restaurants (10-15%) and for taxi drivers (rounding up or 10%). Service charges are sometimes included, so check your bill.

The West End theater district offers world-class shows. Book tickets in advance for popular shows, or try day-of tickets at TKTS booths for discounts.
