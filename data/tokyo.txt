Tokyo, Japan's bustling capital, is a fascinating blend of traditional culture and cutting-edge technology, offering an unforgettable travel experience.

Airports:
Tokyo is served by two major airports: Narita (NRT) and Haneda (HND). Narita is the primary international airport, located about 60 km east of central Tokyo. Haneda is closer, about 15 km south of the city center, and handles both domestic and international flights.

Transportation from airports:
From Narita (NRT), the Narita Express (N'EX) is the most convenient option, taking about 60 minutes to Tokyo Station or Shinjuku. A one-way ticket costs approximately 3,070 yen. The Keisei Skyliner is faster (about 40 minutes) and cheaper (2,470 yen) but goes to Ueno and Nippori stations. Airport limousine buses are available to major hotels and cost around 3,000-3,500 yen. Taxis are expensive, costing 20,000-30,000 yen to central Tokyo.

From Haneda (HND), the Tokyo Monorail connects to Hamamatsucho Station in about 20 minutes for 500 yen. The Keikyu Line provides direct access to Shinagawa Station in about 15 minutes for 410 yen. Airport buses cost around 1,000-1,200 yen to major areas.

Getting around Tokyo:
Tokyo's public transportation system is one of the world's best. The JR Yamanote Line is a loop line connecting major districts like Shibuya, Shinjuku, Harajuku, Ueno, and Tokyo Station. It's essential for navigating the city.

The Tokyo Metro and Toei Subway networks are extensive and cover areas not served by JR lines. A single ride costs 170-310 yen depending on distance.

IC cards like Suica, Pasmo, and Icoca are highly recommended. These rechargeable cards work on virtually all trains, subways, and buses in Tokyo and throughout Japan. You can purchase them at any station for 500 yen (plus your initial charge). They're also accepted at convenience stores, vending machines, and many restaurants.

The Japan Rail Pass is only worth it if you're traveling extensively outside Tokyo. For Tokyo-only travel, IC cards are more economical.

Taxis are available but expensive, with base fares starting around 500 yen. They're useful for short distances or when public transport stops running (most trains stop around midnight).

Major attractions:
Shibuya Crossing is the world's busiest pedestrian intersection. The area is known for shopping, dining, and nightlife. The Hachiko statue is a popular meeting point.

Senso-ji Temple in Asakusa is Tokyo's oldest temple, dating back to 628 AD. The approach is lined with traditional shops selling souvenirs and snacks. Admission is free.

The Tokyo Skytree, at 634 meters, offers breathtaking views of the city. Tickets cost 3,100 yen for the main deck and 4,700 yen for both decks. Advance booking is recommended.

The Imperial Palace and East Gardens are open to the public (reservations required for palace tours). The gardens are free and beautiful, especially during cherry blossom season.

Harajuku is famous for its youth culture, street fashion, and Takeshita Street. It's a vibrant area for shopping and people-watching.

Akihabara is the electronics and anime district, a must-visit for tech enthusiasts and anime fans.

Tsukiji Outer Market (the inner wholesale market moved to Toyosu) still offers excellent fresh seafood, sushi, and Japanese street food.

Accommodation:
Tokyo offers diverse accommodation options. Capsule hotels provide a unique Japanese experience starting around 3,000-5,000 yen per night. Business hotels offer comfortable rooms for 8,000-15,000 yen. Mid-range hotels cost 15,000-30,000 yen, while luxury hotels can exceed 40,000 yen per night.

Popular areas to stay include Shibuya, Shinjuku, Ginza, and Asakusa, each offering different experiences.

Food and dining:
Tokyo is a food lover's paradise with more Michelin-starred restaurants than any other city. Sushi ranges from affordable conveyor belt sushi (kaiten-zushi) at 100-500 yen per plate to high-end omakase experiences costing 20,000+ yen.

Ramen shops are everywhere, with bowls typically costing 800-1,500 yen. Each shop has its own style and flavor profile.

Izakaya (Japanese pubs) offer a casual dining experience with small plates and drinks, typically costing 3,000-5,000 yen per person.

Convenience stores (konbini) like 7-Eleven, FamilyMart, and Lawson offer surprisingly good food, from onigiri (rice balls) to bento boxes, typically 100-500 yen.

Street food in areas like Harajuku and Asakusa includes takoyaki (octopus balls), taiyaki (fish-shaped pastries), and crepes.

Practical tips:
Tokyo is extremely safe, with low crime rates. However, be mindful of your belongings in crowded areas.

Cash is still widely used, though credit cards are increasingly accepted. ATMs at convenience stores and post offices accept foreign cards.

Tipping is not customary in Japan and can be considered rude. Excellent service is expected as standard.

The best times to visit are spring (March-May) for cherry blossoms and fall (September-November) for pleasant weather and autumn colors. Summer is hot and humid, while winter is mild but can be cold.

Many restaurants have vending machine ordering systems. Look for machines near the entrance, select your meal, pay, and give the ticket to staff.

Bowing is an important part of Japanese culture. A slight bow when greeting or thanking someone is appreciated.

Quiet is valued on public transportation. Keep conversations low and phones on silent mode.

Many attractions and restaurants require advance reservations, especially during peak seasons.
