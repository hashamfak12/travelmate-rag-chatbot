Rome, the Eternal City, is a living museum where ancient history, Renaissance art, and modern Italian life coexist beautifully.

Airports:
Rome is served by two main airports: Fiumicino (FCO), also known as Leonardo da Vinci Airport, and Ciampino (CIA). Fiumicino is the main international airport, located about 30 km southwest of the city center. Ciampino is smaller, located about 15 km southeast, and primarily serves low-cost airlines.

Transportation from airports:
From Fiumicino (FCO), the Leonardo Express is a direct train to Termini Station in central Rome, taking about 32 minutes. Tickets cost 14 euros and can be purchased at the airport or online. The FL1 regional train is cheaper (8 euros) but makes stops and takes longer. Airport buses like Terravision and SitBusShuttle cost around 6-8 euros and take 45-60 minutes to Termini. Taxis have a fixed rate of 48 euros to the city center (within the Aurelian Walls).

From Ciampino (CIA), buses like Terravision and SitBusShuttle connect to Termini for around 4-6 euros, taking about 40-50 minutes. Taxis cost approximately 30 euros to the city center.

Getting around Rome:
Rome's public transportation includes buses, trams, and two metro lines (A and B). A single ticket (BIT) costs 1.50 euros and is valid for 100 minutes on all public transport. The Roma Pass includes public transport and museum access, starting at 32 euros for 48 hours.

The metro is useful but limited compared to other major cities, as archaeological discoveries have limited expansion. It's most useful for reaching areas outside the historic center.

Buses and trams cover the city extensively. Be aware that schedules can be irregular, and buses can be crowded.

Walking is often the best way to explore Rome's historic center, where many attractions are within walking distance of each other.

Taxis are available but can be expensive. Official taxis are white with a taxi sign on top. Uber operates but is limited compared to other cities.

Major attractions:
The Colosseum is Rome's most iconic landmark. Advance booking is essential to avoid extremely long queues. A standard ticket (18 euros) includes the Colosseum, Roman Forum, and Palatine Hill and is valid for 24 hours. The underground and arena floor require special tickets.

The Roman Forum and Palatine Hill are adjacent to the Colosseum and offer fascinating ruins of ancient Rome. Allow 2-3 hours to explore properly.

The Pantheon is a remarkable ancient Roman temple with the world's largest unreinforced concrete dome. Admission is free, but it can get very crowded. It's closed on certain holidays.

The Trevi Fountain is a stunning Baroque masterpiece. It's free to visit but always crowded. Tossing a coin in the fountain (with your right hand over your left shoulder) is a tradition said to ensure your return to Rome.

The Spanish Steps connect Piazza di Spagna with Trinità dei Monti church. The area is great for shopping and people-watching.

The Vatican City, an independent city-state, houses St. Peter's Basilica (free admission, but expect queues) and the Vatican Museums, home to the Sistine Chapel. Museum tickets cost 17-21 euros, and advance booking is highly recommended to avoid hours-long queues.

The Borghese Gallery houses an incredible collection of Bernini sculptures and Caravaggio paintings. Advance reservation is mandatory, with tickets costing 13 euros plus a 2 euro reservation fee.

Piazza Navona is a beautiful Baroque square with fountains and cafes, perfect for people-watching.

Accommodation:
Rome offers accommodation for all budgets. Hostels start around 20-40 euros per night. Budget hotels cost 60-100 euros, mid-range hotels 100-200 euros, and luxury hotels can exceed 300 euros per night.

Staying near the historic center (Centro Storico) is convenient but more expensive. Trastevere offers a more local, bohemian atmosphere. The area around Termini Station is budget-friendly but less charming.

Food and dining:
Roman cuisine is hearty and delicious. Classic dishes include carbonara (pasta with eggs, cheese, pancetta, and black pepper), cacio e pepe (pasta with cheese and black pepper), amatriciana (pasta with tomato and guanciale), and saltimbocca alla romana (veal with prosciutto and sage).

A meal at a trattoria typically costs 25-40 euros per person. Pizzerias offer excellent pizza al taglio (by the slice) for 3-5 euros per slice, or sit-down pizza for 8-15 euros.

Gelato is a must-try. Look for artisanal gelaterias (avoid places with brightly colored, piled-high gelato, which often indicates lower quality). Expect to pay 2-4 euros for a cone.

Coffee culture is important. A cappuccino at a bar costs 1-1.50 euros if you stand at the counter, but can cost 3-5 euros if you sit at a table.

Many restaurants close between lunch and dinner (around 3-7 PM). Dinner typically starts around 7:30-8 PM, with locals often dining later.

Practical tips:
Rome uses the Euro. Credit cards are widely accepted, but cash is still common, especially at smaller establishments.

Rome is generally safe, but be extremely vigilant against pickpockets, especially on public transport, at major attractions, and in crowded areas. Keep valuables secure and be aware of common scams.

Many attractions offer free admission on the first Sunday of the month, but expect very large crowds.

The Roma Pass can save money if you plan to visit multiple paid attractions and use public transport frequently.

The best times to visit are spring (April-May) and fall (September-October) when weather is pleasant and crowds are more manageable. Summer is hot, crowded, and expensive. Winter is cooler and less crowded but still pleasant.

Dress codes apply at religious sites. Shoulders and knees should be covered when visiting churches, including St. Peter's Basilica.

Booking major attractions in advance is essential, especially for the Colosseum, Vatican Museums, and Borghese Gallery. This saves hours of waiting in line.

Tipping is not as expected as in the US, but rounding up or leaving 1-2 euros for good service is appreciated. A service charge (coperto) of 1-3 euros per person is often added to restaurant bills.

Many shops and restaurants close for a few hours in the afternoon (riposo), typically from 1-4 PM.
