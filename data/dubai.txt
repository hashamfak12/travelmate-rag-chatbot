Dubai, in the United Arab Emirates, is a modern metropolis known for luxury shopping, ultramodern architecture, and a vibrant nightlife scene.

Airports:
Dubai is served by Dubai International Airport (DXB), one of the world's busiest airports, located about 5 km east of the city center. Dubai World Central (DWC), also known as Al Maktoum International Airport, is located about 37 km southwest and primarily serves cargo and some passenger flights.

Transportation from airport:
The Dubai Metro Red Line connects Dubai Airport to the city center and various destinations. A single journey costs 3-8.50 AED depending on zones. The metro is clean, efficient, and air-conditioned. Airport buses are available, typically costing 5-10 AED. Taxis are metered and cost approximately 50-80 AED to the city center, depending on destination. Uber and Careem (regional ride-sharing) are also available.

Getting around Dubai:
The Dubai Metro has two lines (Red and Green) that cover much of the city. It's modern, air-conditioned, and efficient. Fares range from 3-8.50 AED depending on zones. The Nol card is a smart card used for metro, buses, trams, and water buses.

Buses complement the metro network and cover areas not served by the metro. The bus system is extensive and uses the same Nol card system.

Taxis are plentiful and relatively affordable, with starting fares around 5 AED plus distance charges. They're metered and air-conditioned. Uber and Careem are widely used.

The Dubai Tram serves the Dubai Marina and JBR area. Water buses and water taxis operate on Dubai Creek and along the coast.

Major attractions:
Burj Khalifa is the world's tallest building at 828 meters. The observation decks on the 124th and 148th floors offer stunning views. Tickets start around 149 AED for the 124th floor, with higher prices for the 148th floor. Advance booking is essential.

The Dubai Mall is one of the world's largest shopping malls, with over 1,200 stores, an aquarium, an ice rink, and numerous restaurants. It's adjacent to the Burj Khalifa and the Dubai Fountain.

The Dubai Fountain show takes place outside the Dubai Mall and Burj Khalifa, with water, music, and light displays. Shows occur every 30 minutes in the evening. It's free to watch.

Palm Jumeirah is an artificial island shaped like a palm tree, home to luxury hotels including Atlantis The Palm. The Palm Monorail connects the mainland to the island.

The Dubai Marina offers modern skyscrapers, restaurants, and a beautiful waterfront promenade. It's a great area for walking and dining.

The Gold Souk and Spice Souk in Deira offer traditional markets where you can buy gold, spices, and other goods. Haggling is expected.

The Dubai Museum, located in the Al Fahidi Fort, provides insight into Dubai's history and transformation. Admission is around 3 AED.

Accommodation:
Dubai offers a wide range of accommodation, from budget options to ultra-luxury resorts. Budget hotels start around 150-250 AED per night. Mid-range hotels cost 300-600 AED, while luxury hotels can exceed 1,000 AED per night.

The city is known for its luxury resorts, many located on the Palm Jumeirah or along the beach. Popular areas include Downtown Dubai (near Burj Khalifa), Dubai Marina, JBR, and the historic Deira/Bur Dubai area.

Food and dining:
Dubai's food scene is incredibly diverse, with cuisine from around the world. Traditional Emirati food includes shawarma, falafel, hummus, and grilled meats. Lebanese, Indian, Pakistani, and Iranian cuisines are particularly popular.

A meal at a casual restaurant typically costs 40-80 AED. Fine dining restaurants can cost 200-500+ AED per person. The city has many celebrity chef restaurants and Michelin-starred establishments.

Street food is available and affordable, with shawarma and falafel typically costing 10-20 AED.

Alcohol is available in licensed restaurants and bars, usually in hotels. It's expensive due to taxes.

Practical tips:
Dubai uses the UAE Dirham (AED). Credit cards are widely accepted, and contactless payment is common.

The best times to visit are November to March when weather is pleasant (20-30°C). Summer (June-September) is extremely hot (40-50°C) and humid, with most activities moving indoors.

Dubai is very safe with low crime rates. However, be respectful of local customs and laws. Public displays of affection, drinking in public, and inappropriate dress can result in fines or legal issues.

Dress modestly, especially when visiting mosques or traditional areas. While beachwear is acceptable at beaches and pools, cover up when leaving these areas.

Tipping is appreciated but not mandatory. 10-15% at restaurants is common if service charge isn't included.

Many attractions offer online booking with discounts. The Dubai Pass can save money if visiting multiple paid attractions.

The work week is Sunday to Thursday, with Friday and Saturday being the weekend. Many businesses and attractions adjust their hours accordingly.

Ramadan affects opening hours and dining options. Many restaurants close during daylight hours, and eating or drinking in public is prohibited during fasting hours.
