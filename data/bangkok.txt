Bangkok, Thailand's vibrant capital, is a city of contrasts where ancient temples stand alongside modern skyscrapers, and traditional markets coexist with luxury malls.

Airports:
Bangkok is served by two main airports: Suvarnabhumi (BKK) and Don Mueang (DMK). Suvarnabhumi is the main international airport, located about 30 km east of the city center. Don Mueang is older and primarily serves low-cost airlines and domestic flights, located about 24 km north of the city.

Transportation from airports:
From Suvarnabhumi (BKK), the Airport Rail Link (ARL) is the fastest and cheapest option, connecting to Phaya Thai station in about 25-30 minutes for 45 baht. From Phaya Thai, you can transfer to the BTS Skytrain. Airport buses connect to various parts of the city for 150 baht. Taxis use meters and cost approximately 300-400 baht to central Bangkok, plus a 50 baht airport surcharge and tolls (around 25-70 baht). Always insist on using the meter.

From Don Mueang (DMK), the easiest option is usually a taxi (200-300 baht plus surcharge and tolls) or ride-share service. Public transport connections are less convenient.

Getting around Bangkok:
Bangkok's traffic is notorious, so public transport is often faster than taxis during peak hours. The BTS Skytrain (elevated train) and MRT (underground metro) are the most efficient ways to get around. Fares range from 17-62 baht depending on distance. Both systems use contactless cards or single-journey tokens.

The Chao Phraya Express Boat is a scenic and practical way to travel along the river, serving areas like Wat Pho, Wat Arun, and the Grand Palace. Fares range from 15-32 baht depending on the boat type.

Tuk-tuks are iconic three-wheeled vehicles, but negotiate the price before getting in as they don't use meters. They're fun for short trips but can be overpriced for tourists.

Taxis are plentiful and cheap if they use the meter (starting at 35 baht). Always insist on "meter" when getting in. Traffic can be heavy, so allow extra time.

Motorcycle taxis are fast for short distances but can be dangerous. Only use them if you're comfortable with the risk.

Major attractions:
The Grand Palace is Bangkok's most famous attraction, a stunning complex of royal buildings and temples. It's open daily 8:30 AM-3:30 PM, with tickets costing 500 baht. The adjacent Wat Pho (Temple of the Reclining Buddha) is also a must-see, with tickets costing 200 baht. Both can get extremely crowded, so arrive early.

Wat Arun (Temple of Dawn) is a beautiful riverside temple, especially stunning at sunset. Tickets cost 100 baht. It's accessible by ferry from Tha Tien pier.

Wat Saket (Golden Mount) offers panoramic views of the city. Climbing the 318 steps is rewarded with great views. Admission is 50 baht.

Chatuchak Weekend Market is one of the world's largest markets, with over 15,000 stalls selling everything from clothing to antiques to food. It's open Saturday and Sunday, and getting there early (around 9 AM) helps avoid the heat and crowds.

Floating markets like Damnoen Saduak (touristy but iconic) and Amphawa (more authentic) offer a glimpse into traditional Thai life. Tours are available, or you can visit independently.

Lumpini Park is a green oasis in the city center, perfect for jogging, people-watching, or escaping the urban hustle. It's free and open daily.

The Jim Thompson House showcases traditional Thai architecture and art. The American entrepreneur's former home is now a museum. Tickets cost 200 baht.

Accommodation:
Bangkok offers excellent value for accommodation. Budget hostels start around 300-600 baht per night. Mid-range hotels cost 1,500-3,000 baht and often include pools and good amenities. Luxury hotels can be found for 4,000-10,000+ baht per night, often with exceptional service and facilities.

Popular areas to stay include Sukhumvit (convenient, good restaurants and nightlife), Silom (business district, good transport links), Khao San Road (backpacker area, budget-friendly), and riverside (luxury hotels with great views).

Food and dining:
Bangkok is a food paradise. Street food is safe, delicious, and incredibly cheap. Pad Thai costs 40-60 baht, som tam (papaya salad) 30-50 baht, and grilled meat skewers 10-20 baht each. Look for busy stalls with locals - that's usually a sign of good, fresh food.

Tom yum goong (spicy shrimp soup) and green curry are must-try dishes. A meal at a local restaurant typically costs 100-200 baht.

Food courts in malls offer air-conditioned comfort and a wide variety of options, typically 50-150 baht per dish.

Fine dining restaurants offer excellent value compared to Western cities, with high-quality meals costing 500-2,000 baht per person.

Fruit is abundant and delicious. Try mango sticky rice (khao niao mamuang), a popular dessert.

Practical tips:
Bangkok uses the Thai Baht (THB). Cash is still widely used, though credit cards are accepted at hotels, restaurants, and larger establishments. ATMs are everywhere and accept foreign cards (usually charging a 220 baht fee per transaction).

Bangkok is generally safe, but be aware of common scams targeting tourists, especially around major attractions. Be cautious of overly friendly strangers offering unsolicited help or "closed temple" stories trying to redirect you to gem shops or travel agencies.

Dress modestly when visiting temples. Shoulders and knees should be covered. You can often rent or borrow appropriate clothing at temple entrances.

The weather is hot and humid year-round, with the cool season (November-February) being the most pleasant. The rainy season (June-October) brings frequent but usually brief downpours.

Tipping is not expected but appreciated for good service. Rounding up taxi fares or leaving 20-50 baht at restaurants is common.

Bargaining is expected at markets and with tuk-tuk drivers, but not at department stores or restaurants with fixed prices. Start at about 50-60% of the asking price and negotiate from there.

The best times to visit are during the cool season (November-February) when weather is most pleasant. Songkran (Thai New Year in April) is a fun but very crowded time to visit, with city-wide water fights.

Many attractions can get extremely crowded, especially the Grand Palace. Arriving early (before 9 AM) helps avoid the worst crowds and heat.

Traffic can be terrible, especially during rush hours (7-9 AM and 5-7 PM). Plan accordingly and use public transport when possible.

Tap water is not safe to drink. Stick to bottled water, which is cheap and widely available.
