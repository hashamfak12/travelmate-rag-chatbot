Mexico City, the capital of Mexico, is one of the world's largest cities, known for its rich history, vibrant culture, and excellent food scene.

Airports:
Mexico City is served by Mexico City International Airport (MEX), also known as Benito Juárez International Airport, located about 13 km east of the city center. It's Mexico's busiest airport.

Transportation from airport:
The metro connects to the airport (Terminal Aérea station on Line 5), costing 5 MXN. It's the cheapest option but can be challenging with luggage. Airport buses connect to various parts of the city for around 70-100 MXN. Taxis from authorized stands (sitio taxis) cost approximately 200-400 MXN to the city center, depending on zone. Uber is widely available and often safer and more convenient. Always use authorized transport from the airport.

Getting around Mexico City:
The Mexico City Metro is extensive, cheap (5 MXN per journey), and covers much of the city. However, it can be crowded, especially during rush hours. The system uses tokens or rechargeable cards.

Metrobús is a bus rapid transit system with dedicated lanes, making it faster than regular buses. Fares are around 6 MXN.

Regular buses cover areas not served by metro or Metrobús. They're cheap but can be confusing for tourists.

Taxis should be from authorized stands or called by phone/app. Uber is very popular and often safer than street taxis. Always verify you're getting into the correct vehicle.

The city is large and spread out, so walking between neighborhoods isn't always practical.

Major attractions:
The Historic Center (Centro Histórico) is a UNESCO World Heritage Site with the Zócalo (main square), the Metropolitan Cathedral, and the Templo Mayor (Aztec ruins). The area is rich in history and architecture. Many attractions are free or low-cost.

The Templo Mayor Museum showcases Aztec artifacts and ruins. Admission is around 80 MXN.

The National Museum of Anthropology is one of the world's great museums, with an incredible collection of pre-Columbian artifacts. Admission is around 85 MXN, free on Sundays for Mexican nationals.

Chapultepec Park is a large park with museums, a zoo, and the Chapultepec Castle. The park is free, though individual attractions charge admission.

Frida Kahlo Museum (Casa Azul) in Coyoacán is the former home of the famous artist. It's very popular, so advance booking is recommended. Admission is around 250 MXN for foreigners.

Xochimilco is famous for its colorful trajineras (gondola-like boats) on ancient canals. It's a unique experience, especially on weekends. Boat rides cost around 500-600 MXN per hour for a boat.

Teotihuacan, the ancient city of the pyramids, is located about 50 km northeast of the city. It's a must-see day trip. Entry is around 80 MXN. Tours are available, or you can take public transport.

Accommodation:
Mexico City offers good value accommodation. Hostels start around 200-400 MXN per night. Budget hotels cost 600-1,200 MXN, mid-range hotels 1,200-2,500 MXN, and luxury hotels can exceed 4,000 MXN per night.

Popular areas to stay include Centro Histórico (central, historic), Roma and Condesa (trendy, good restaurants), Polanco (upscale, safe), and Coyoacán (bohemian, cultural).

Food and dining:
Mexico City is a food paradise, with some of the world's best street food and restaurants. Tacos are everywhere and delicious, typically costing 10-30 MXN each. Try al pastor, carnitas, and barbacoa.

Street food is excellent and safe if you choose busy stalls with high turnover. Tacos, quesadillas, elotes (corn), and tamales are popular. A meal can cost 50-150 MXN.

A meal at a casual restaurant typically costs 150-300 MXN. Fine dining restaurants can cost 500-1,500+ MXN per person. The city has many excellent restaurants, including several on the World's 50 Best Restaurants list.

Markets like Mercado de San Juan and Mercado Roma offer diverse food options.

Practical tips:
Mexico City uses Mexican Pesos (MXN). Credit cards are widely accepted, though cash is still common, especially for street food and small purchases. ATMs are available, but be cautious and use those in secure locations.

The best times to visit are October to April when weather is pleasant and dry. May to September is rainy season, with afternoon showers common. The city is at high altitude (2,240 meters), so it can be cool, especially at night.

Mexico City is generally safe in tourist areas, but be cautious. Use authorized taxis or Uber, avoid displaying valuables, be aware of your surroundings, especially at night, and avoid certain areas. Consult locals or your accommodation for advice.

The altitude can cause mild altitude sickness. Stay hydrated and take it easy the first day or two.

Traffic can be heavy, and air quality can be poor. The metro is often faster than road transport.

Tipping is expected: 10-15% at restaurants, and small tips for services are appreciated.

Spanish is the language, though English is spoken in tourist areas and hotels. Learning basic Spanish phrases is very helpful.

Many museums offer free entry on Sundays for Mexican nationals. Some have free entry for everyone on certain days.

The city is large and spread out. Plan your itinerary to minimize travel between distant neighborhoods.

Be cautious with street food if you're not used to it. Start with cooked items, choose busy stalls, and avoid raw vegetables and water from unknown sources. Drink bottled water.

Tap water is not safe to drink. Use bottled water for drinking and brushing teeth.
